## About
I am a former Spanish translator and recent Master of Science graduate in Human Language Technology from the University of Arizona. My academic and professional background combines applied linguistics, natural language processing (NLP), and machine learning. I have a particular interest in developing ethical language technologies that are practical for real-world applications.

I was born and raised in a border city in northwestern Mexico. Before pursuing graduate studies, I earned a bachelor's degree in Translation from the Universidad Autónoma de Baja California. I completed coursework in the major branches of linguistics in both English and Spanish, as well as translation theory and practice. In 2019, I interned at the Université de Moncton in Canada, on an international research project focused on the acquisition and teaching of Latin American Spanish as a second language. The project’s findings were later presented and published in a peer-reviewed academic journal in Mexico. In December 2020, I was the recipient of the "Mérito Escolar" award (Valedictorian) for achieving the highest GPA in a graduating cohort of 20+ students. Soon after graduation, I began working in the healthcare industry as a medical translator and interpreter at Valleywise Health Medical Center in Phoenix, Arizona. This experience strengthened my communication, organization, and problem-solving skills while giving me firsthand insight into the importance of timely access to language technologies that facilitate communication in critical health situations.

While I worked at Valleywise Health, I decided I wanted to change careers but lacked the required expertise in computer science. Hence, I took programming and advanced algebra courses at Scottsdale Community College, where I learned to code in Python and polished my mathematical skills. My first experience in the field of AI came when I began working as a data annotator at Innodata Inc. This role gave me a deeper understanding of large language models and served as my first exposure to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). During graduate school, I expanded my programming skills and gained knowledge in data science and computer science through coursework. I expanded my programming skills by learning R and SQL and became proficient in machine learning frameworks including PyTorch, TensorFlow, Hugging Face Transformers, and scikit-learn. I also learned to use Git and containerization platforms like Docker to develop and manage coding projects.

Throughout the Human Language Technology program, I completed several courses in the College of Information Science, including Applied Natural Language Processing, Data Mining, and Neural Networks, with the goal of developing interdisciplinary skills that prepare me for a career in AI. These courses provided extensive experience with machine learning and deep learning methods for analyzing large-scale data, building predictive models, and extracting meaningful insights. I gained practical experience in model design, hyperparameter tuning, and performance evaluation. These projects were among the most rewarding aspects of the program, as they gave me the opportunity to apply classroom concepts to real-world problems. For one of my courses, I built a convolutional neural network (CNN) for multi-label emotion classification using TensorFlow and Keras. The model optimized the micro F1 score, selected the best classification threshold, and generated predictions for unseen data. Additionally, in my Advanced Statistical NLP course, I compared a supervised Transformer-based machine translation system with a prompted decoder-only language model, evaluating translation quality using BLEU and chrF metrics while conducting detailed error analyses to understand the strengths and limitations of each approach. I also trained a small automatic speech recognition (ASR) model for my Speech Technology course, where I explored decoding strategies to reduce hallucinations and improve transcription stability for a low-resource language.

During my final year of graduate school, I interned with NeuralSeek, where I developed an AI agent that assists non-technical users with their mobile devices using the company's in-development platform, mAIstro. I currently work as a Spanish Medical Annotation Subject Matter Expert at Centific, where I review, transcribe, and annotate multilingual data used to train large language models for medical applications. These experiences have given me a stronger understanding of how language technology is developed in practice, including the importance of data quality, collaboration, clear documentation, and aligning technical work with real project needs.

The projects on this website represent the areas I have focused on throughout the HLT program, including NLP, machine learning, conversational AI, speech technology, and software development. Together, they show how I have applied programming and language technology to different problems and how my interests have developed from academic projects into real-world AI applications.
